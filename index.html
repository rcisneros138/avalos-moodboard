What they have in common

Saturated blue + hot orange

All three sites and the Zohran poster use this pairing. It's now the standard look for left-wing campaigns in New York. Copying it would make Amanda look like a Zohran knockoff in Milwaukee.

Heavy headlines, some hand-lettered

Every reference leads with bold, blocky type that reads from a car. The two images go further: drop shadows, stacked layers and brush script, the language of hand-painted signs.

Language access as part of the brand

Claire puts Español in the main nav. The Zohran system was built to work in seven languages. For a district that's about 70% Hispanic, bilingual design needs to be there from the start.

One accent color, used sparingly

Orange shows up as punctuation, never as body text. White text on that orange fails contrast. That's fine on a big sign and a problem on a web button.

Draft palettes for Amanda

Four directions. Each takes the energy of the references and grounds it in Milwaukee's South Side instead of New York. Contrast ratios are WCAG; 4.5:1 is the minimum for body text.

Before anything gets printed
  • Decide who approves the final look (likely Shua and Amanda). It sets message consistency across lit, mail, web, social and signs.
  • Test the chosen palette in one-color and two-color print. That's how most yard signs and door lit get printed cheaply.
  • Pick free Google Fonts (Archivo, as used here, or similar) so volunteers can use them in Canva without buying a license.
